\section{Why this document}

The Social Networks special-issue rejection (Bianchi's follow-up email) said:

\begin{quote}\itshape
``the underdevelopment of the behavioural model assumed in your ABM. While it can certainly
be considered an agent-based model, the behaviour of the agents is relatively abstract, and
the experimental design appears to be governed by macro-level manipulation rather than
bottom-up dynamics.''
\end{quote}

Two pieces of the current model are exposed to that critique:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The network threshold $\tau_i$ is exogenous.} It is drawn from
    $\mathcal{N}(\tau_{\text{mean}}, \tau_{\text{sd}})$, truncated to $[0,1]$,
    \emph{independently of the agent's own measured preference} $q_i$. The sweep over
    $(\tau_{\text{mean}}, \tau_{\text{sd}})$ is exactly the ``macro-level manipulation''
    Bianchi points at: a population-level dial with no micro-foundation in the survey data.
  \item \textbf{The two channels are bolted together.} Rational adoption ($q_i \le \Gamma$)
    and social adoption ($\tau_i \le \Ee_i$) are two separate rules joined by an OR. A
    referee can ask why an agent runs two unrelated decision procedures.
\end{enumerate}

Tur, Zeppini \& Frenken (2024, \emph{Social Networks} 76:12--21) --- published in our
target journal --- has a \emph{single} micro rule (a utility comparison, where social
reinforcement enters the utility calculus), but \textbf{no social-distance sensitivity in
influence}: every adopting neighbor reinforces equally. NDFU's distinctive ingredient is
exactly that sensitivity (Centola 2011). The question is therefore:

\begin{quote}
Can we keep NDFU's selective (distance-sensitized) influence while adopting a Tur-style
single utility-based decision rule --- making the model bottom-up without giving up its
identity?
\end{quote}

Answer: yes. The instrument is a single \textbf{unified adoption rule}
(\S\ref{sec:unified}), which admits two operationalizations --- a
\emph{linear-fractional} variant and a \emph{multiplicative $\gamma$-variant} that nests
Tur (2024) exactly. Their pros and cons are compared in detail in
\S\ref{sec:proscons}.

\section{Notation and the current NDFU model (baseline)}
\label{sec:notation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Source \\
\midrule
$\Gamma \in [0,1]$ & Intrinsic Utility Level (IUL) of the behavior & swept \\
$q_i \in [0,1]$ & Minimum Utility Requirement (MUR): intrinsic aversion & \textbf{empirical} (GSS/ATP items) \\
$\tau_i \in [0,1]$ & network threshold (fraction of influential adopters needed) & \textbf{exogenous} $\mathcal{N}(\tau_{\text{mean}}, \tau_{\text{sd}})$ \\
$d_{ij} \in [0,1]$ & Gower distance in Blau space (age, educ, race, relig, sex) & \textbf{empirical} \\
$h \in [0,1]$ & Maximum Social Proximity (MSP): tolerance to dissimilarity & swept (macro) \\
$a_j \in \{0,1\}$ & adoption state of $j$ & dynamic \\
$X_{ij}$ & adjacency & imputed ERGM networks \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Current rules (as in the paper).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rational channel: adopt if $q_i \le \Gamma$ (Eq.~1 of the paper).
  \item Social channel: adopt if $\tau_i \le \Ee_i$, with selective effective exposure
\begin{equation}
  \Ee_i \;=\; \frac{\sum_{j\neq i} X_{ij}\,\atil_j}{\sum_{j\neq i} X_{ij}},
  \qquad
  \atil_j = 1 \;\iff\; a_j = 1 \,\wedge\, U_{ij} \le \sigma(h - d_{ij}),
  \label{eq:exposure}
\end{equation}
  where $U_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$ is redrawn per step and
  $\sigma(z) = \bigl(1 + e^{-z/T}\bigr)^{-1}$ with $T = 0.02$ in the implementation
  (a near-hard gate at $d_{ij} = h$; see \code{scripts/04\_GSS\_diffusion\_sims.R},
  line~304).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation nuance worth knowing} (it matters for the comparison with Tur):
in the code, the rational channel is implemented by setting the node's threshold to
$10^{-6}$, so a rational agent still needs \textbf{one influential contact} (exposure
$>0$) before adopting. The engine is therefore already a word-of-mouth model for
\emph{awareness}: low-$q$ agents behave as simple contagion, not as spontaneous
($\varphi = 0$) adopters. The paper's Eq.~1 (adoption at exposure $0$) is the
idealization; the code is contact-gated.

\section{Tur (2024) mechanism, for reference}
\label{sec:tur}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Word-of-mouth \textbf{percolation}: agents must be \emph{informed} through the
    network before they can decide.
  \item Single decision rule: adopt when the innovation's intrinsic utility $\xi$ meets
    the agent's minimum utility requirement.
  \item \textbf{Social reinforcement modifies the requirement} --- exact published form
    (confirmed against both PDFs):
\begin{equation}
  u_t^i \;=\; u_0^i \cdot \left(\frac{1}{a_t^i}\right)^{\gamma},
  \label{eq:turform}
\end{equation}
    where $u_0^i$ is the initial minimum utility requirement, $a_t^i \in \mathbb{N}$ the
    number of \textbf{adopting neighbors} of $i$ at time $t$ (a state count, not received
    recommendations; the rule is evaluated only once informed, so $a \ge 1$), and
    $\gamma \ge 0$ the social-reinforcement strength. The lowering is
    \textbf{multiplicative} (power-law): each doubling of adopting neighbors divides the
    requirement by the same factor $2^{\gamma}$. With $\gamma = 0$ the model reduces to
    pure social percolation. The threshold-like behavior emerges from this lowering, not
    from a separate threshold parameter.
  \item Homophily $\rho$ is purely \textbf{structural pre-wiring}: agents with similar
    $u$ are placed together \emph{before} diffusion. Influence itself is
    \textbf{distance-blind}: any adopting neighbor reinforces equally.
  \item Preferences are artificial distributions ($\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ family in
    2018; uniform in 2024); networks are Watts--Strogatz with $N = 10{,}000$ and
    $k = 4$ neighbors on average.
\end{itemize}

NDFU vs.\ Tur, in one line: \textbf{Tur has the single bottom-up rule but uniform
influence; NDFU has selective influence but a two-channel rule with an exogenous
$\tau$.} Each has what the other lacks.

\section{The unified additive-utility rule}
\label{sec:unified}

\subsection{The rule}

Replace both channels by one utility comparison per agent per step:
\begin{equation}
  \text{adopt}_i(t) = 1 \;\iff\; \Gamma + \lambda \, \Ee_i(t) \;\ge\; q_i .
  \label{eq:unified}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Ee_i(t)$ is \textbf{exactly the current selective effective exposure}
    \eqref{eq:exposure}, unchanged. This is where social distance enters: only adopters
    passing the stochastic proximity gate $\sigma(h - d_{ij})$ count.
  \item $\lambda > 0$ is the \textbf{social-proof coupling}: how much situational utility
    a fully-adopted, fully-similar neighborhood adds, in the same units as $\Gamma$ and
    $q_i$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Two algebraically identical framings (choose the second)}
\label{sec:framings}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Requirement-lowering (Tur's framing):}
    $q_i^{\eff}(t) = q_i - \lambda \Ee_i(t)$; adopt when $\Gamma \ge q_i^{\eff}(t)$.
    Preferences are \emph{modified} during diffusion.
  \item \textbf{Situational-utility (recommended framing):}
    $U_i(t) = \Gamma + \lambda \Ee_i(t)$; adopt when $U_i(t) \ge q_i$. The preference
    $q_i$ \textbf{never changes}; what changes is the utility the situation offers (peers
    add legitimacy, lower uncertainty, network externalities). This is literally
    Granovetter's threshold as a \emph{situated cost--benefit crossing} (1978, p.~1422),
    and it preserves NDFU's declared contrast with Tur (``preferences are not modified
    during diffusion'', \code{theoretical\_reviewed.md}, entry~7).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The derived threshold --- the two old channels fall out as limit cases}
\label{sec:derived}

Rearranging \eqref{eq:unified}, adoption requires $\Ee_i(t) \ge \tau_i^{*}$ with the
\textbf{derived threshold}
\begin{equation}
  \tau_i^{*} \;=\; \max\!\left\{0,\; \frac{q_i - \Gamma}{\lambda}\right\}.
  \label{eq:taustar}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $q_i \le \Gamma \;\Rightarrow\; \tau_i^{*} = 0$: adopts at first influential
    contact --- \textbf{the current rational channel} (Granovetter's low-threshold
    ``instigator'', exactly as currently implemented with $\tau = 10^{-6}$).
  \item $q_i > \Gamma \;\Rightarrow\; \tau_i^{*} > 0$: needs selective exposure
    proportional to the \textbf{utility deficit} --- the social channel, now derived
    instead of postulated.
  \item $q_i > \Gamma + \lambda \;\Rightarrow\; \tau_i^{*} > 1$: \textbf{can never adopt}
    ($\Ee \le 1$) --- a ``structurally immune'' agent. When exactly this occurs is worked
    out in \S\ref{sec:immunity}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this buys and what it costs}
\label{sec:buys}

\paragraph{Buys.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bottom-up microfoundation.} One decision rule, all heterogeneity
    empirical: $\tau_i^{*}$ is a deterministic function of the \emph{measured} $q_i$ and
    the product $(\Gamma, \lambda)$. The arbitrary
    $\mathcal{N}(\tau_{\text{mean}}, \tau_{\text{sd}})$ disappears. This is the direct
    answer to Bianchi.
  \item \textbf{Behavioral realism as a bonus:} resistance to peer influence is now
    positively correlated with intrinsic aversion (the same latent trait drives both) ---
    defensible, and testable in principle.
  \item \textbf{Nesting.} Setting the gate to $w \equiv 1$ ($h \to \infty$,
    distance-blind) removes the distance selectivity and leaves a Tur-style
    (distance-blind) reinforcement model; setting $\lambda \to 0$ recovers a pure
    percolation-of-awareness model. Tur (2024)'s \emph{exact} published form
    \eqref{eq:turform} is recovered by the $\gamma$-variant of the rule --- see
    \S\ref{sec:turnesting}. NDFU's selectivity remains the \emph{identified added
    ingredient}, cleanly positioned against a paper published in the target journal.
  \item \textbf{Implementability.} $\tau_i^{*}$ is static given $(\Gamma, \lambda)$, so
    the existing \code{netdiffuseR} threshold machinery runs it as-is:
    \code{threshold.dist = pmax(0, (q - $\Gamma$)/$\lambda$)} replaces the Normal draw
    $+$ the $10^{-6}$ hack. No engine changes needed.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Costs / changes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The rational/social decomposition becomes \textbf{ex-post bookkeeping} (adopters
    with $q_i \le \Gamma$ vs.\ $q_i > \Gamma$) rather than two mechanisms --- same tally
    we already compute, but the interpretation shifts.
  \item $\tau_{\text{sd}}$ disappears as a free dial; its large positive effect on
    cascades (heterogeneity creates susceptible minorities) is now carried by the
    \emph{empirical} dispersion of $q_i$, scaled by $1/\lambda$. $\lambda$ replaces
    $(\tau_{\text{mean}}, \tau_{\text{sd}})$ --- the parameter space shrinks from 16
    ($4\times4$) threshold configurations to a 1-D $\lambda$ sweep.
  \item The (IUL $\times$ MSP) heatmaps will not be identical: thresholds now co-vary
    with $q$. Whether the phase structure survives is an empirical question --- answered:
    it does (see \S\ref{sec:premiumresult}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where social distance enters (explicitly)}

Only inside $\Ee_i$, via the per-contact stochastic gate:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\bigl(\text{$j$'s adoption counts for $i$}\bigr) \;=\; \sigma\!\left(\frac{h - d_{ij}}{T}\right),
  \qquad T = 0.02 .
\end{equation}
Identical to the current model. The unified rule changes the \emph{decision rule}, not
the \emph{influence filter}.

\subsection{Recovering Tur (2024) exactly --- the nesting, step by step}
\label{sec:turnesting}

\paragraph{Tur's published rule} (exact form, \S\ref{sec:tur}): an agent that has been
\emph{informed} through the network adopts when the product's utility meets its
\emph{current} requirement,
\begin{equation}
  \xi \;\ge\; u_t^i, \qquad u_t^i = u_0^i \cdot \left(\frac{1}{a_t^i}\right)^{\gamma},
\end{equation}
with $u_0^i$ the initial minimum utility requirement, $a_t^i \ge 1$ the number of adopting
neighbors of $i$ at time $t$ (the rule is only evaluated once informed, so $a \ge 1$ and
the expression is well-defined), and $\gamma \ge 0$ the social-reinforcement strength.
$\gamma = 0$ gives $u_t = u_0$: pure social percolation (Solomon \& Weisbuch 2000), Tur's
own baseline.

\paragraph{Log-linearization --- Tur IS an additive requirement-lowering rule, in logs.}
Taking logarithms (all quantities positive):
\begin{equation}
  \ln \xi \;\ge\; \ln u_0^i \;-\; \gamma \,\ln a_t^i .
  \label{eq:logtur}
\end{equation}
So the published reinforcement is additive with the concave gain $g(a) = \ln a$. This
dictates the right unified rule: it must be additive in \emph{log-utility}, i.e.\
\textbf{multiplicative in levels}.

\paragraph{The Tur-exact unified rule ($\gamma$-form).} Define the distance-sensitized
adopting \emph{count}
\begin{equation}
  \Acount_i(t) \;=\; \sum_{j} X_{ij}\, \atil_j(t),
  \qquad
  \atil_j = 1 \;\iff\; a_j = 1 \,\wedge\, U_{ij} \le \sigma\!\left(\frac{h - d_{ij}}{T}\right),
  \label{eq:count}
\end{equation}
and let social proof \emph{multiply} the situational utility:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\text{adopt}_i(t) \;\iff\; \Gamma \cdot \bigl(\Acount_i(t)\bigr)^{\gamma} \;\ge\; q_i\;}
  \qquad \text{(evaluated when } \Acount_i \ge 1\text{)}.
  \label{eq:gammaform}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in Tur's own requirement-lowering form:
$q_{i,t} = q_i \cdot (1/\Acount_i(t))^{\gamma}$, adopt when $\Gamma \ge q_{i,t}$. The
preference $q_i$ is fixed; what the similar adopters change is the \emph{situational}
requirement --- the same framing as \S\ref{sec:framings} (but see
\S\ref{sec:preferences} for the substantive difference this form does carry).

\paragraph{Recovery, in one step.} Switch off the proximity gate
($h \ge 1 + 5T \Rightarrow \sigma \to 1 \Rightarrow \atil_j = a_j$, so
$\Acount_i(t) = a_t^i$), and rule \eqref{eq:gammaform} reads
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma \cdot (a_t^i)^{\gamma} \ge q_i
  \quad\iff\quad
  \xi \;\ge\; u_0^i \cdot \left(\frac{1}{a_t^i}\right)^{\gamma}
\end{equation}
under the identification
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\xi = \Gamma, \qquad u_0^i = q_i, \qquad \text{same } \gamma\;.}
\end{equation}
The recovery is \textbf{exact on any graph}: Tur's reinforcement is a pure count, and the
$\gamma$-form uses the count directly --- no degree normalization is involved, so no
degree-regularity caveat is needed (unlike the linear-fractional variant, which
normalizes by degree and matches Tur's count reading only on degree-regular substrates).

\paragraph{Awareness / percolation layer} (unchanged): the engine's contact-gating (the
$\tau = 10^{-6}$ device, \S\ref{sec:notation}) means the rule is only evaluated at
$\Acount_i \ge 1$ --- precisely Tur's ``informed'' condition with per-contact
transmission probability~1; a probability $p < 1$ is emulated by a constant-acceptance
gate ($\sigma \equiv p$).

\paragraph{Derived threshold (count form).} Rearranging \eqref{eq:gammaform}:
\begin{equation}
  \text{adopt}_i \;\iff\; \Acount_i(t) \;\ge\; c_i^{*},
  \qquad
  c_i^{*} = \left(\frac{q_i}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma}.
  \label{eq:cstar}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $q_i \le \Gamma \Rightarrow c_i^{*} \le 1$: adopts at the first influential
    contact --- the rational channel, exactly as in the linear variant
    (\S\ref{sec:derived}).
  \item $q_i > \Gamma \Rightarrow$ needs $c_i^{*}$ similar adopters; heterogeneity 100\%
    empirical (from $q_i$), with $\gamma$ as the single coupling.
  \item \textbf{Degree-dependent immunity}: agent $i$ is out of reach whenever
    $c_i^{*} > k_i$ (its degree) --- high-aversion, \emph{low-degree} agents can never be
    socially converted (see \S\ref{sec:immunity}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What does NOT nest (honesty box).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Tur's homophily $\rho$ sorts agents by the requirement $u$ \emph{itself}; NDFU's
    imputation sorts by demographics, and $q_i$ correlates with demographics only
    partially. The imputed networks are therefore an \emph{empirically disciplined,
    weaker} version of Tur's preference-sorting. The nesting claim concerns the
    \textbf{adoption rule}; the network is an exogenous input in both models.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{One-line summary.} With the proximity gate switched off, the $\gamma$-form
\textbf{is} Tur (2024) --- same functional form, same $\gamma$, with $\xi = \Gamma$ and
$u_0 = q$ --- on any topology; NDFU's added ingredient is exactly one: the distance gate
deciding \emph{which} adopters count in $\Acount_i$.

\section{Pros and cons of the two operationalizations}
\label{sec:proscons}

We now have two members of one family on the table:
\begin{align}
  \text{\textbf{Linear-fractional:}} \qquad & \Gamma + \lambda\, \Ee_i(t) \;\ge\; q_i
  && \text{(additive in the adopted \emph{fraction})}
  \label{eq:linvar}\\[2pt]
  \text{\textbf{$\gamma$-form (Tur-exact):}} \qquad & \Gamma \cdot \Acount_i(t)^{\gamma} \;\ge\; q_i
  && \text{(multiplicative in the adopted \emph{count})}
  \label{eq:gamvar}
\end{align}
This section works through the five questions that decide between them, pedagogically
and one at a time, and closes with a verdict table.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{When can an agent never adopt? (immunity, made precise)}
\label{sec:immunity}

In the linear variant, immunity is $\tau_i^{*} > 1$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  q_i \;>\; \Gamma + \lambda .
\end{equation}
Since $q_i \le 1$, this region is \textbf{non-empty only when} $\lambda < 1 - \Gamma$:
with $\lambda \ge 1$ immunity is impossible (a fully-adopted similar neighborhood can
compensate any deficit). So the intuition ``$q_i, \Gamma \in [0,1]$ makes this very
unlikely'' is correct \emph{at large $\lambda$} but not in general. Empirically, computed
directly from $q_i = \code{mur\_score}$ on the 96 GSS networks (the same 1000 agents in
every network, so the shares do not vary across networks; GSS mean $q = 0.585$; isolates
0.11\%), share of agents with $q_i > \Gamma + \lambda$:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccc@{}}
\toprule
$\lambda$ & $\Gamma = 0$ & $0.1$ & $0.2$ & $0.3$ & $0.4$ & average over the IUL grid \\
\midrule
0.5 & 66.4\% & 46.5\% & 32.2\% & 16.1\% & 6.2\% & 14.0\% \\
0.6 & 46.5\% & 32.2\% & 16.1\% & 6.2\% & 0 & 8.1\% \\
0.7 & 32.2\% & 16.1\% & 6.2\% & 0 & 0 & 4.1\% \\
0.8 & 16.1\% & 6.2\% & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1.7\% \\
0.9 & 6.2\% & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0.4\% \\
1.0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The share depends only on $\Gamma + \lambda$: 66.4, 46.5, 32.2, 16.1 and 6.2\% for
$\Gamma + \lambda = 0.5, \dots, 0.9$, and zero from $1$ on.

The immune share is only an \textbf{upper bound} on adoption, $\Phi \le 1 - \Pr(q_i >
\Gamma + \lambda) - \text{isolates}$, and with the correct $q$ the dynamics stay far below
it at low IUL. At $\lambda = 0.5$ (random seeding) the highest adoption anywhere on the MSP
axis is 3--4\% for $\Gamma \le 0.1$ (the seed cluster plus a few), 8\% at $\Gamma = 0.2$
(bound 68\%) and 27\% at $\Gamma = 0.3$ (bound 84\%), and jumps to 85--88\% at
$\Gamma = 0.4$ (bound 94\%): most non-immune agents still need a large adopting share of
their neighborhood ($\tau_i^{*} = (q_i - \Gamma)/\lambda$ close to~1), and the cascade
stalls before the immune tail binds. The bound is a prediction, not a bug: \emph{when
social proof is weak, its reach is bounded}. The current engine cannot produce it because
$\tau$ is truncated to $[0,1]$ by construction. (Under the inverted $q$ the immune tail was
10.9\% at $\lambda = 0.5$, $\Gamma = 0.2$, and showed as a visible ceiling
$\Phi \approx 0.75$.)

In the $\gamma$-form, immunity is instead $c_i^{*} > k_i$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{q_i}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/\gamma} > k_i
  \quad\iff\quad
  q_i \;>\; \Gamma \, k_i^{\gamma},
\end{equation}
which depends on the agent's \textbf{degree}: the out-of-reach agents are those both
highly averse \emph{and} poorly connected. Arguably more realistic --- and a distinctive,
testable difference between the two variants.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The log-space bridge: $\ln a$ vs.\ $\Ee$}
\label{sec:logbridge}

In log space, Tur's reinforcement variable is $\ln a$ --- the log of the gated
\emph{count}. Is that ``just'' our normalized exposure? Almost: with
$\Ee_i = \Acount_i / k_i$,
\begin{equation}
  \ln \Acount_i \;=\; \ln \Ee_i \;+\; \ln k_i .
  \label{eq:logdecomp}
\end{equation}
On \textbf{degree-regular substrates} --- and Tur's Watts--Strogatz networks are
degree-homogeneous with $k = 4$ --- the term $\ln k_i$ is one global constant, absorbable
into $\Gamma$ (or $u_0$): identifying the reinforcement with $\ln \Ee$ is then exact up
to recalibration. On NDFU's \textbf{heterogeneous} networks (degrees roughly 1--80), the
two differ by the agent-specific offset $\gamma \ln k_i$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item using $\Ee$ says ``reinforcement is a \emph{share of your attention}'';
  \item using $\Acount$ says ``every endorsement adds the same, whether you have 4
    neighbors or 40'' --- at the same adopted \emph{fraction}, a hub receives more
    reinforcement.
\end{itemize}
This is the count-vs-fraction fork of \S\ref{sec:turnesting}, seen from the logarithm:
the analogy $\ln a \leftrightarrow \Ee$ is complete \emph{modulo degree}, and the choice
between the two variants is precisely the choice of what to do with $\ln k_i$.

\subsection{What $\gamma$ is --- and is not --- in Tur 2018 and Tur 2024}
\label{sec:gammastatus}

Read against both PDFs, the epistemic status of $\gamma$ is the following.

\paragraph{What their justification does have.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An \textbf{axiomatic characterization} (2018, p.~3; restated in 2024): the
    updated requirement $f(q; a, \gamma) = q \cdot (1/a)^{\gamma}$ must satisfy
    (a) decreasing in $a$ for $\gamma > 0$; (b) decreasing in $\gamma$ for $a > 1$;
    (c) $\gamma = 0$ replicates the percolation benchmark; (d) $a = 1$ gives $f = q$
    (the \emph{first} adopting neighbor only informs; reinforcement starts with the
    second --- a clean echo of complex contagion's ``more than one source'').
  \item A \textbf{concavity argument} for the restriction $\gamma \le 1$: ``assuming
    that the marginal effect of each additional neighbor on the total social influence
    cannot be increasing'' (2018), with the reductio that a very large $\gamma$
    trivializes diffusion \emph{on their degree-4 lattices}.
  \item An interpretable \textbf{marginal effect} (2018, Eq.~2):
    $\Delta q_t^i = -\, q_0^i \gamma \,/\, (a_t^i)^{\gamma+1} \cdot \Delta a$ --- the
    first adopting friends produce the large drops, and the drop is \emph{proportional
    to the agent's own requirement} (more averse agents receive larger absolute
    reductions). This proportionality is the seed of the complementarity discussed in
    \S\ref{sec:preferences}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What it does not have.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No empirical calibration.} In both papers $\gamma$ is a free
    ``intensity'' parameter, introduced with one sentence (2024: ``for some innovations,
    social reinforcement may be more intense than for others \dots\ so we introduce
    social reinforcement as a parameter'') and \emph{swept}: $\gamma \in [0,1]$
    continuously in the 2018 phase maps and the 2024 heat maps, with focal values
    $\{0, 0.5, 1\}$ in 2024's Fig.~5 (``$\gamma = 1$ is maximum social reinforcement'').
    There are no units, no measurement strategy, no experimental anchor --- not even a
    link to Centola's dose--response evidence, which would have been the natural one.
  \item \textbf{No transportability across degree scales.} All Tur simulations live on
    $k = 4$ neighborhoods, so $a \in \{1, \dots, 4\}$ in practice and the reinforcement
    spans at most a factor $4^{\gamma} \le 4$. On NDFU's imputed networks (mean degree
    $\approx 29$, hubs beyond 60), the same $\gamma$ produces reductions up to
    $29^{\gamma}$: at $\gamma = 1$ a saturated average neighborhood divides the
    requirement by $\approx 29$, a force of a different order of magnitude. Even their
    $\gamma \le 1$ bound is reasoned \emph{for} $k = 4$. \textbf{Borrowing Tur's
    $\gamma$ values would inherit the symbol, not the calibration.}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verdict.} Adopting the $\gamma$-form buys (i) exact formal nesting, (ii) the
published axioms and the concavity argument for free, and (iii) a citation in the target
journal. It does \emph{not} buy empirical values for $\gamma$, nor freedom from arguing
the coupling's magnitude --- if the $\gamma$-form is used, $\gamma$ must be calibrated
for \emph{our} degree distribution (e.g.\ with the same fit-to-current-model procedure
used for $\lambda$). Epistemically, $\gamma$ and $\lambda$ are in the same place: free
couplings --- with the difference that $\lambda$ has the stronger microeconomic backing
(\S\ref{sec:brockdurlauf}).

\subsection{Exposure traditions: the power form is wedded to counts}
\label{sec:exposuretrad}

The $\gamma$-form cannot be written with normalized exposure, and the reason is
structural, not stylistic: the multiplier $\Acount^{\gamma}$ amplifies only because
$\Acount \ge 1$. Substituting the fraction $\Ee \in [0,1]$ gives
$\Ee^{\gamma} \le 1$ --- social proof would \emph{reduce} situational utility. There is
no natural ``fractional $\gamma$-form''.

Consequences:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Choosing the $\gamma$-form moves the model from the fractional-threshold
    tradition (Granovetter 1978; Valente 1996; Watts 2002) --- the tradition NDFU and
    \code{netdiffuseR} live in --- to the absolute-count tradition (Centola \& Macy
    2007). For a \emph{Social Networks} audience this is not neutral.
  \item \textbf{Mitigation 1 (mechanism $\ne$ measurement):} even if the decision rule
    runs on counts, Valente-style exposure-at-adoption can still be computed and
    reported \emph{ex post} as a descriptive statistic (measured thresholds). Nothing
    forbids reporting $\Ee_i$ at the moment of adoption.
  \item \textbf{Mitigation 2 (tooling):} \code{netdiffuseR} supports non-normalized
    exposure (\code{exposure()} with \code{normalized = FALSE}), so the count threshold
    $\lceil (q_i/\Gamma)^{1/\gamma} \rceil$ runs on the same machinery. The loss is
    conceptual continuity, not implementation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Are preferences modified? Composition, not units}
\label{sec:preferences}

Formally, the $\gamma$-form does \emph{not} force the ``preferences change'' reading:
$\Gamma \cdot \Acount^{\gamma} \ge q_i$ is symmetric, and the factor
$\Acount^{\gamma}$ can be booked on either side ---
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\;U_i(t) = \Gamma \cdot \Acount_i^{\gamma} \ge q_i\;}_{\text{situational
  utility multiplied; preference fixed}}
  \qquad\iff\qquad
  \underbrace{\;\Gamma \ge q_i \cdot \Acount_i^{-\gamma} = q_{i,t}\;}_{\text{requirement
  lowered (Tur's framing)}}
\end{equation}
with identical behavior. In logs the first reading is a Cobb--Douglas-style utility in
two ``goods'' (product quality, social proof): $\ln U_i = \ln \Gamma + \gamma \ln
\Acount_i$. Both sides live on the same $[0,1]$ utility scale as $q_i$, so the real
distinction is not one of \emph{units} but of \textbf{composition}: additive vs.\
multiplicative.

And the composition carries substance. Compare the marginal value of social proof:
\begin{align}
  \text{linear:}\qquad
  & U_i = \Gamma + \lambda \Ee_i,
  & \frac{\partial U_i}{\partial \Ee_i} &= \lambda,
  & \frac{\partial^2 U_i}{\partial \Gamma\, \partial \Ee_i} &= 0
  && \text{(\textbf{substitutes})},\\[4pt]
  \text{$\gamma$-form:}\qquad
  & U_i = \Gamma \cdot \Acount_i^{\gamma},
  & \frac{\partial U_i}{\partial \Acount_i} &= \gamma\, \Gamma\, \Acount_i^{\gamma - 1},
  & \frac{\partial^2 U_i}{\partial \Gamma\, \partial \Acount_i} &= \gamma \Acount_i^{\gamma-1} > 0
  && \text{(\textbf{complements})}.
\end{align}
In the linear form, a given (similar) adopting neighborhood contributes the same
$\lambda \Ee$ regardless of how good the product is: quality and social proof are
\emph{substitutes} (additively separable), and the social contribution needed to convert
agent $i$ compensates the utility deficit one-for-one, $\lambda \Ee_i \ge q_i - \Gamma$,
independently of $\Gamma$'s level. In the $\gamma$-form the cross-partial is strictly
positive (complementarity in the Edgeworth--Pareto sense): \emph{the same adopting
neighborhood is worth more for better products}. The mirror statement on the requirement
side is Tur 2018's own Eq.~2, where the drop is proportional to $q_0^i$. The required
social proof falls steeply with quality,
\begin{equation}
  \ln c_i^{*} \;=\; \frac{\ln q_i - \ln \Gamma}{\gamma},
  \qquad
  \frac{d \ln c_i^{*}}{d \ln \Gamma} \;=\; -\frac{1}{\gamma},
\end{equation}
an elasticity of $-1/\gamma$ --- e.g.\ at $\gamma = 0.5$, doubling the product's quality
divides the required count of similar adopters by four.

So it \emph{can} be framed as preferences-fixed either way, but the multiplicative
interaction is a substantive behavioral commitment (quality--social-proof
complementarity), not just bookkeeping --- and since Tur themselves present the form as
requirement-lowering, adopting their exact form and citation invites the ``preferences
change'' reading unless a paragraph defends the other bookkeeping.

\paragraph{The prediction that tells them apart.} The social proof needed to convert $i$
depends on the \emph{difference} $q_i - \Gamma$ under the linear form and on the
\emph{ratio} $q_i / \Gamma$ under the $\gamma$-form. Because $q_i$ and $\Gamma$ live on
a survey-index scale, the distinction is cardinal: in logs the $\gamma$-form is additive
too (\S\ref{sec:turnesting}). What is scale-free is the shape of the indifference curves
between quality and social proof: parallel lines (linear) or hyperbolas that blow up as
$\Gamma \to 0$ ($\gamma$-form). Which composition is right is a question about the
\emph{mechanism} by which peers matter; \S\ref{sec:mechanisms} reads the literature on
it.

\subsection{Substitutes or complements? What the mechanisms imply}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

Until 2026-11 the only support for the linear form was the modelling convention of Brock \&
Durlauf (2001). This subsection reads twelve papers against the question of
\S\ref{sec:preferences}: does a given adopting neighborhood contribute the same whatever the
quality (substitutes), or more for better products (complements)? The organizing frame is
Young (2009, AER 99(5):1899--1924), who sorts diffusion models by \emph{why} prior adoption
matters: contagion, social influence (conformity) and social learning. The working
hypothesis was: if social proof is a payoff for conforming it adds (substitutes); if it is
information about quality it combines multiplicatively (complements), which would suggest
substitutes for the collective action of the GSS and complements for the technology of the
ATP. The reading confirms the first half and corrects the second.

\paragraph{Conformity and coordination: every model adds, in shares.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Bass (1969, Mgmt.\ Sci.\ 15(5)), pp.~216--217: the hazard of a first purchase is
    $p + q F(T)$, ``a linear function of the number of previous buyers''.
  \item Morris (2000, RES 67(1)), pp.~58, 60; Ellison (1993, Econometrica 61(5)), p.~1049;
    Blume (1993, GEB 5(3)), p.~391 and Eq.~(2.3): each player sums the payoffs of a
    $2\times2$ coordination game over her neighbors, so action 1 is a best response when the
    \emph{proportion} of neighbors playing it exceeds a critical value set by the payoffs.
    That is the derived threshold \eqref{eq:taustar}, obtained rather than assumed.
  \item Brock \& Durlauf (2001, RES 68(2)), Eq.~(1), p.~238: private plus social utility
    plus a shock. The authors adopt the parametric forms because they ``render our model
    both theoretically and econometrically tractable'' (p.~238), and their
    ``complementarity'' ($\partial^2 S/\partial\omega_i\partial\bar m = J$, Eq.~3) is between
    one's action and the others', not between quality and social proof. NDFU is their
    model at zero temperature with heterogeneous private utility $h_i = \Gamma - q_i$, a
    case they allow (\S5.3, Eq.~37).
  \item Soetevent (2006, J.\ Econ.\ Surveys 20(2)), pp.~197, 200: the linear form is
    \emph{assumed} in the empirical literature, and the behavioral motive is almost never
    identified. P.~222 proposes, as an unexplored extension, interaction weights that depend
    on the characteristics of both parties, $\gamma_{ij} = \gamma(x_i, x_j)$: NDFU's
    similarity gate.
  \item Young (2009), p.~1905: in the social-influence model adoption depends on
    popularity, ``rather than on how good or desirable the innovation has proven to be''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Learning does not yield Tur's product.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer \& Welch (1992, JPE 100(5)): agents adopt if
    $E[V\mid\text{history}] \ge C$ (p.~996). Evidence adds in log-odds --- the difference
    between adoptions and rejections is a sufficient statistic (n.~23, p.~1009) --- and it
    \emph{saturates}: once a cascade starts further adoptions are uninformative, whereas
    under conformity uniformity ``becomes more robust as the number of adopters increases''
    (p.~1004).
  \item Young (2009), Eqs.~(13), (16), pp.~1911--1912: under learning from outcomes the
    evidence $i$ needs is her prior deficit divided by the true margin of quality over her
    cost. There is complementarity, but agents whose cost exceeds the true quality never
    adopt; Young analyses only $\{i: \mu > c_i\}$ (p.~1912).
\end{itemize}
Tur's $\Gamma\cdot\Acount^{\gamma}$ cannot be learning: it is unbounded in the count, and
learning can never persuade an agent that a product is better than it is. It is a reduced
form, with axioms but no mechanism (\S\ref{sec:gammastatus}).

\paragraph{The experiments establish the channel, not the functional form.} Salganik,
Dodds \& Watts (2006, Science 311) show that a social signal makes success more unequal and
the quality--success mapping more convex (Fig.~3), but this is an aggregate market-share
result. Krumme et al.\ (2012, PLoS ONE 7(5)), p.~2, separate sampling (where the social
signal and appeal \emph{add}) from downloading (driven by quality alone); the product is
\emph{between} stages, which NDFU already has as its awareness condition. Cai, Chen \& Fang
(2009, AER 99(3)) find that a top-five ranking raises demand by 13--20\% (Tables 3, 5), more
so among infrequent customers (Table 8), and cannot separate learning from conformity
(n.~8, p.~865). Tucker \& Zhang (2011, Mgmt.\ Sci.\ 57(5)) find that popularity helps
narrow-appeal vendors more, with an additive utility (p.~829). \textbf{None of them estimates
the sign of $\partial^2 U / \partial\Gamma\,\partial\Ee$ at the individual level.} (Manski
1993 is cited only through Brock \& Durlauf and Soetevent.)

\paragraph{Where quality lives (our derivation from Morris's game).} In a two-action game
with each neighbor, the advantage of adopting is always $-a + (a + b)\,\Ee$, with $a$ the loss
from adopting when the neighbor did not and $b$ the gain when she did. It is linear in the
share. If quality is value that does not need others, it lowers $a$ and shifts the line:
\emph{substitutes}. If quality is value realized only jointly (a network good, a protest that
needs a crowd), it raises $b$ and tilts the line: \emph{complements, bounded and in shares},
$\Gamma + (\lambda_0 + \varphi\Gamma)\Ee \ge q$, not Tur's form.

\subsection{The Brock--Durlauf microfoundation of the linear form}
\label{sec:brockdurlauf}

The linear variant is the deterministic limit of the canonical
discrete-choice-with-social-interactions model of Brock \& Durlauf (2001,
\emph{Review of Economic Studies} 68(2):235--260). In their setup, agent $i$ chooses a
binary action and derives utility
\begin{equation}
  V_i(\omega_i) \;=\;
  \underbrace{h_i(\omega_i)}_{\text{private utility}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{J\, \omega_i\, \bar{m}_i^{e}}_{\text{social utility (conformity)}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\varepsilon_i(\omega_i)}_{\text{random shock}},
  \label{eq:bd}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{m}_i^{e}$ is the expected mean choice of $i$'s \emph{reference group} and
$J \ge 0$ is the social-interaction (conformity) coefficient. Three points of contact:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Additive separability of private and social utility is the standard
    assumption} of that literature --- exactly the structure of the linear rule --- adopted
    for tractability (p.~238); the substantive reasons for it are in
    \S\ref{sec:mechanisms}. Mapping:
    the private net utility of adopting is $\Gamma - q_i$; the social term is
    $\lambda \Ee_i$, with $\Ee_i$ playing $\bar{m}_i^{e}$ (the adoption rate of the
    reference group) and $\lambda$ playing $J$. Adoption when total utility is
    non-negative:
    $\;\Gamma - q_i + \lambda \Ee_i \ge 0\;$ is \eqref{eq:unified} rearranged.
  \item \textbf{NDFU's additions are well-defined deviations, not violations:} the
    reference group is not exogenous but \emph{similarity-gated} (the $\sigma(h -
    d_{ij})$ filter selects who belongs to $i$'s effective reference group --- our
    contribution), and the shock is degenerate ($\varepsilon \to 0$), turning
    Brock--Durlauf's logit choice probabilities into a deterministic threshold rule.
    Reintroducing a small $\varepsilon$ would give a smooth (logit) adoption rule --- a
    natural robustness variant.
  \item \textbf{$J$ has an econometric life that $\gamma$ lacks.} The Brock--Durlauf
    coefficient underpins the social-multiplier / social-interactions estimation
    literature (see Blume, Brock, Durlauf \& Ioannides 2011, \emph{Handbook of Social
    Economics}, for identification), so $\lambda$ inherits a path toward empirical
    anchoring. As a bonus, Brock \& Durlauf themselves develop the mean-field
    correspondence of \eqref{eq:bd} with the Ising model --- which connects this
    microfoundation directly to NDFU's criticality analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical status: the coupling \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda} and the premium}
\label{sec:calibration}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$\lambda$ is a free coupling, swept, not calibrated.} The study sweeps $\lambda \in \{0.5, 0.6,
    \dots, 1.0\}$ and reports every result as a function of $\lambda$
    (\S\ref{sec:premiumresult}). Under the linear-fractional variant
    $\tau_i^{*} = \max\{0, (q_i - \Gamma)/\lambda\}$, so the implied threshold
    distribution depends on $\Gamma$, $\lambda$ and the empirical $q$ distribution.
  \item \textbf{Why this range.} $\lambda = 1$ is the \textbf{immunity boundary}: since
    $q \le 1$, $\lambda \ge 1$ leaves no structurally immune agent at any $\Gamma > 0$,
    while $\lambda < 1$ leaves an immune share $\Pr(q > \Gamma + \lambda)$
    (\S\ref{sec:immunity}). At $\lambda = 0.5$ two thirds of the agents are already
    immune at $\Gamma = 0$. There is no theoretical bound at $\lambda = 1$ ($\lambda$ is a utility
    contribution, like Brock--Durlauf's unbounded $J$).
  \item \textbf{Retired: the calibration against the legacy engine.} An earlier sweep over
    $\lambda$ from 0.3 to 1.5 (random seeding) found a best fit to the legacy surface ($\tau \sim \mathcal{N}(0.40, 0.12)$, $\beta_{\text{DP}} = -0.850$) at
    $\lambda \approx 0.7$. Both engines read the propensity score as $q$ (the inverted-$q$
    bug), so it compared two invalid surfaces, and the study does not need a calibrated
    $\lambda$. Neither $\lambda \approx 0.7$ nor $-0.850$ should be cited (retired
    2026-11-02).
  \item The mirror $\gamma$-form sweep (count threshold $(q_i/\Gamma)^{1/\gamma}$ on the
    same machinery) was run on 2026-11-10 (\code{06f}, \S\ref{sec:mechanisms}). The
    ATP/innovation system is swept since PR \#4 (\S\ref{sec:pr4}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Seed matching.} The primary seed is drawn once per run, independently of
$\lambda$. For \emph{random}, \emph{marginal} and \emph{central} it is the \emph{same node}
in a GSS network and its DP twin; \emph{closeness} and \emph{eigen} use each topology's
own most central node. The seed cluster is the primary plus its nearest nodes,
$0.40 \times$ the network's maximum degree in total ($\approx 28$ seeds). A network and
its twin share the degree sequence, so the cluster has the same size in both (checked in
all 96 runs of every strategy), and the strategies differ only in where they seed. With a
low-degree primary the cluster reaches into the second neighborhood, so its shape
differs too, not only its center. The \emph{random} strategy is the reference.

\paragraph{Uncertainty: the run is the unit.} The 1025 cells of a run share the GSS
network, its DP twin and the primary draw, so they are not independent observations:
the unit of replication is the \textbf{run} (96 per topology). Run $k$ also uses the same
network, twin and primary draw in every strategy, so contrasts between strategies are
\textbf{paired}. All intervals come from a run-level bootstrap: resample \code{run\_id}
(1..96) with replacement, with one set of weights shared by GSS, DP and every strategy,
and refit ($B = 1000$; 95\% percentile intervals). The model, per $\lambda$, is a joint BAM
\begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{logit} \Phi \;=\; \alpha_s + \beta_{\text{DP},s}\,\mathbf{1}_{\text{DP}}
  + f_s(\Gamma, h), \qquad f_s = \code{te}(\Gamma, h, k = 12 \times 12)\ \text{per strategy } s,
\end{equation*}
fitted on run-aggregated counts (exact: the model has no run term). Three choices,
decided by checking them against the data:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The surface varies by strategy.} With one shared surface, each strategy's
    misfit leaks into the strategy~$\times$~topology terms and flattens the contrasts (at
    $\lambda = 1$, central $-$ random shrinks from $-0.21$ to $-0.08$). With
    strategy-specific surfaces the joint model factorizes and reproduces the separate
    per-strategy fits exactly; what it adds is the paired bootstrap of the contrasts.
  \item \textbf{It does not vary by topology.} $\beta_{\text{DP}}$ is \emph{defined} as
    the constant logit shift between GSS and DP. A topology-specific surface would absorb
    the premium itself. The premium is really a shift of the tipping frontier, and its
    shape is described by the link-free gap and the $MSP_c$ shift, not by the BAM.
  \item \textbf{Basis $12 \times 12$, not $k = 10$.} The $s(\Gamma, h, k = 10)$ surface of
    05/06 is too stiff for the sharp frontier (deviance explained 0.94--0.99). $\beta_{\text{DP}}$
    converges at a $12 \times 12$ tensor basis; $k = 10$ understates $|\beta|$ by
    3--13\%, more at high $\lambda$. Contrasts between strategies barely move. $k = 10$
    values are kept for continuity.
\end{itemize}
The pooled premium is the unweighted mean of the six per-$\lambda$ estimates
(``$\lambda$-mean''). The pooled fit of 05 has one intercept per $\lambda$ but puts one
surface under all of them, and is attenuated (random: $-0.718$ vs.\ $-0.763$ with the same
$k = 10$ basis; an earlier version also left out the $\lambda$ intercepts and gave $-0.684$).

\paragraph{Reading.} \textbf{The premium survives the bottom-up engine across the whole
$\lambda$ band and for all five seeding strategies, and no interval comes near zero}:
rewiring the ties lowers the odds of adoption by 39--67\% under random seeding

per decile $-0.131$, $-0.203$, $-0.137$, $-0.083$, $-0.063$): the stronger social proof is,

standard errors for the hub strategies and 64--220 times for random and marginal, whose

$\lambda$ is a free coupling, not a calibrated constant, so the paper should report
results as functions of $\lambda$. Two scales must be read together:
\begin{itemize}
  \item on the \textbf{odds} scale the premium more than doubles over the band
    ($-0.50 \to -1.12$; $-0.48 \to -0.98$ with the $k = 10$ surface);
  \item on the \textbf{probability} scale it grows by $+65\%$ (4.0 $\to$ 6.6 pp), with
    much tighter intervals and no dependence on the surface basis.
\end{itemize}
Part of the difference is a link effect (cells with $\Phi > 0.95$ go from 40\% to 61\% of
the plane for GSS, from 32\% to 48\% for DP, and the logit inflates a given probability gap
there), but most of the growth is genuine structural separation.

\paragraph{The $\lambda$-robust statements} (recommended for the paper):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Where it lives.} The premium is concentrated in an L-shaped band along
    the tipping frontier: 18--22\% of the (IUL, MSP) plane, where GSS gains 16--26

    topologies agree (both dead, or both saturated). Structure decides where the system
    is undecided.
  \item \textbf{Tipping shift.} DP needs 1.8--1.9 times the social openness to tip
    ($MSP_c \approx 0.077$--$0.088$ for GSS vs.\ $0.147$--$0.157$ for DP; ratio
    $\lambda$-mean $1.87$ $[1.81, 1.89]$). The absolute shift is flat in $\lambda$:
    0.069--0.071 under random seeding, $\lambda$-mean $0.070$ $[0.065, 0.073]$. Its

    strategies cannot be told apart on this scale ($\lambda$-mean from $0.065$

    in $\lambda$ ($\beta = -0.146$ $[-0.152, -0.138]$ at every $\lambda$; 1.81 pp

\paragraph{A reverse-premium corner (conditional, not universal).} In a small region ---
high openness ($h \gtrsim 0.6$, median 0.83) and moderate attractiveness
($\Gamma \approx 0.275$--$0.45$) --- \textbf{DP beats GSS}. To tell it from noise, each
cell gets the paired $z$ of its gap over the 96 runs, and counts as a \textbf{robust DP win}
only if it clears a one-sided \emph{simultaneous} threshold: max-$T$ over the 1025 cells of
its strategy $\times$ $\lambda$ panel, familywise 5\%, from the same run bootstrap
($z < -3.6$ to $-3.8$, against $-2$ pointwise; \code{premium\_reverse\_corner.csv}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robust at $\lambda = 0.5$--$0.7$, identically in all five strategies.}
    47--48, 59--61 and 38 corner cells clear the threshold, including \emph{every} cell
    where DP wins by more than 2 pp (2.6--3.4\%, 2.4--2.6\% and 0.8\% of the plane, up to
    7.0, 5.8 and 3.0 pp). At $\lambda = 0.5$ it spills just past the box
    ($\Gamma = 0.475$--$0.525$, $\le 1$ pp).
  \item \textbf{Detectable but negligible at $\lambda = 0.8$} (12--13 corner cells,
    $\le 0.14$ pp, plus 8 cells at $\Gamma = 0.25$ with $\le 0.35$ pp); at
    $\lambda \ge 0.9$ at most 0.04 pp remains.
  \item \textbf{The low-IUL strip} ($\Gamma \le 0.1$, DP ahead by $\le 0.05$ pp) is noise
    for the hub strategies and random: no cell clears the threshold (up to 19 pass
    pointwise). For marginal it is systematic (30--48 cells per $\lambda$, $h \gtrsim
    0.5$) but negligible ($\le 0.14$ pp): an effect of the seed cluster's geometry, not a
    reversal of the premium.
\end{itemize}
The corner tracks the immunity boundary. Its strong part ($> 2$ pp) lies at
$\Gamma + \lambda \approx 0.85$--$0.975$, where a small immune tail remains (13 to 1\% of
agents), and the robust cells drift to lower $\Gamma$ as $\lambda$ grows
($\Gamma = 0.35$--$0.45$ at $\lambda = 0.5$, $0.275$--$0.375$ at $0.7$, $0.275$--$0.3$ at
$0.8$). There the most averse non-immune agents need nearly all of their neighbors to
adopt ($\tau_i^{*}$ close to~1). The mechanism is Tur (2024)'s own: when the gate is wide
open, homophily \emph{isolates the resistant among the resistant}, whereas random mixing
seats adopters next to reluctant agents and converts them. Once influence stops being
similarity-gated, the sign of the structural effect can flip (the similarity-vs-diversity
question). This is a lead, not an established mechanism (it did not hold under the inverted
$q$). The corner keeps the premium claim properly conditional, but it is a weak-coupling
feature and should not be presented as present at every $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Immunity under the correct $q$.} Agents with $q_i > \Gamma + \lambda$ can never
adopt. At $\Gamma = 0$ they are 66, 47, 32, 16, 6 and 0\% of agents for
$\lambda = 0.5, \dots, 1.0$; averaged over the IUL grid, 14.0, 8.1, 4.1, 1.7, 0.4 and 0\%.
Mean adoption over the plane is correspondingly lower (random seeding:
$\bar\Phi_{\text{GSS}} = 0.61$--$0.74$, $\bar\Phi_{\text{DP}} = 0.57$--$0.68$).

\paragraph{What changed with respect to the inverted $q$.} Until 2026-10-05 the engine read
the propensity score itself as $q_i$ (high = \emph{more} willing), the opposite of its role
as a minimum utility requirement (GSS mean 0.415 instead of 0.585); the same inversion
affected the legacy engine. With the correct $q$: (i) the premium survives in every
strategy and keeps its shape (L-shaped band, flat rational channel, stable tipping shift
of $\approx 0.065$--$0.07$); (ii) it is smaller on the odds scale ($\lambda$-mean with
$k = 10$: central $-1.07 \to -0.88$, random $-0.81 \to -0.76$) and, relative to its
level, steeper in $\lambda$ ($\beta$ at $\lambda = 1$ over $\lambda = 0.5$: central
$2.0 \to 2.3$, random $1.6 \to 2.0$); (iii) the reverse corner moves to higher $\Gamma$ (from $0.10$--$0.275$, by
about the 0.17 shift in mean $q$) and is no longer present at every $\lambda$; (iv) the
immune share rises 2.7-fold or more (IUL-grid average 5.2 $\to$ 14.0\% at $\lambda = 0.5$).
The inverted-$q$ outputs are archived in \code{output/**/\_qinverted/} (closeness and eigen
references: the pre-seed-fix runs in \code{\_prefix/}, which match the seed-matched code
for the deterministic strategies: central $-0.926$ vs.\ $-0.928$). Those runs predate the
common seed budget: except for central, whose cluster is the same under both rules, they
seeded $0.40 \times$ the degree of the primary, so central is the like-for-like comparison.

\paragraph{Still open.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What the intervals cover.} The bootstrap treats the 96 networks as
    exchangeable draws from the fitted ERGM. They share the same 1000 imputed agents (the
    $q$ composition is fixed), so the intervals cover tie formation, the seed draw and the
    dynamics, not survey sampling of the agents or uncertainty in the ERGM coefficients.
  \item \textbf{Specification.} The level of $\beta_{\text{DP}}$ depends on the surface
    basis (3--13\% between $k = 10$ and te $12 \times 12$). That is not in the bootstrap
    intervals; the contrasts are robust to it, and the link-free gap, which does not depend
    on it, should lead in the paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{PR \#4: calibrated networks, frozen gate and the exposure axis}
\label{sec:pr4}

The results of \S\ref{sec:premiumresult} were computed on the 96 ERGM networks of
\code{02\_GSS\_network\_ergm.R} with the annealed gate. Branch \code{rcpp-engine} (PR \#4,
stacked on PR \#3) changes three things and keeps the rest (the rule, $q = $
\code{mur\_score}, the $41 \times 25$ grid, $\lambda \in \{0.5, \dots, 1.0\}$, the five
seeding strategies and the common seed budget):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{the networks}: calibrated by race to the GSS 2004 ego networks (level x1),
    then rewired to twice the triangles of every node (level x2, the main networks);
  \item \textbf{the gate}: frozen (one coin per tie and simulation) instead of annealed;
  \item \textbf{the exposure}: the main text keeps the base exposure $E_i = a_i/d_i$; a
    robustness axis with one formula and two dials goes to the supplement.
\end{enumerate}
The same recipe builds networks for the GSS 2004 sample (collective action) and for the
ATP 2014 sample (new products). ATP has no ego networks, so its networks use the GSS 2004
targets, as the ERGM of 02 used the GSS-based coefficients of Smith et al.\ (2014).

\subsection{Networks: calibrated by race, then twice the triangles of every node}
\label{sec:networks}

\paragraph{What was wrong with the ERGM networks.} The ERGM of 02 is dyad independent with
one race coefficient and no degree term. Its degree by race is an artifact (whites 33.9,
blacks 16.8, Hispanics 10.0, Asians 7.5 contacts, against GSS 2004 ratios of confidants
1.09\,:\,0.68\,:\,0.90\,:\,0.84), it has almost no closure (transitivity 0.065, about twice the
density), and its 100 networks share the same 1000 agents.

\paragraph{Level x1} (\code{scripts/02\_network\_calibrated.R}). For every network the 1000
nodes are resampled with replacement from the survey's complete cases. Ties are independent
coins with
\begin{equation*}
  \operatorname{logit} p_{ij} = \delta_{r(i)} + \delta_{r(j)} + \gamma_{r}\,\mathbf{1}[r(i) = r(j) = r]
  + \beta^{\top} x_{ij},
\end{equation*}
where $\beta^{\top} x_{ij}$ holds the sex, religion, age and education terms of Smith et al.\
(2014), the activity $\delta$ (five groups) and the homophily $\gamma$ (White, Black,
Hispanic, Asian) are solved by damped Newton so that the \emph{expected} degree sum and the
expected within-group ties of each group equal the GSS 2004 targets for the network's own
nodes: mean degree 30 (Boase et al.\ 2006) times the relative number of confidants, and
the share of same-race confidants. This moment matching is the maximum likelihood estimate
of the dyad-independent model (what \code{ergm.ego} estimates for
\code{nodefactor} $+$ \code{nodematch(race, diff = TRUE)}); it converges in 9--11 iterations
to a relative gap below $10^{-6}$. ``Other'' (18 GSS respondents; 1.1\% of the GSS nodes,
2.4\% of the ATP nodes) is not calibrated: its degree target ($0.49$ $[0.25, 0.79]$ of the
mean) is too noisy and homophily inside a residual category is not meaningful, so it gets
relative degree 1 and the common race coefficient. All four calibrated groups reproduce
their targets inside the 95\% bootstrap intervals (\code{02c\_network\_calibrated\_tests.R};
in ATP the ratios between groups are those of the GSS, while degree relative to the ATP
mean shifts with the composition).

\paragraph{Level x2.} Degree-preserving swaps $(a,b),(c,d) \to (a,d),(c,b)$ that keep the
race mixing matrix and the numbers of same-sex and same-religion ties \emph{exactly} and
the total age and education differences within 1\%, accepted (Metropolis) toward twice the
triangles of \emph{every} node of x1, $\sum_i |t_i - 2\,t_i^{x1}|$, and then $5 \times
10^7$ more proposals that keep that error small while the network mixes
(\code{02\_closure\_rewire.cpp}, \code{rewire\_closure\_node()}; a few seconds per network). The
per-node error ends at 1.0\%. x1 and x2 have the same nodes, degrees and mixing; they
differ only in closure:

\paragraph{Why every node, not the total.} The first version of the rewiring targeted the
total number of triangles (now level x2g, \code{--closure-mode=global}). The cheapest
triangles are among high-degree nodes, so x2g concentrated them there: local clustering
0.08 in the lowest degree quintile and 0.21 in the highest, degree assortativity
$0.33 \to 0.46$. The held-out GSS 2004 ego networks reject that profile
(\S\ref{sec:validation}): closure among confidants is flat to falling with the size of the
ego network, while in x2g it rises by 16--28 points. Doubling every node's triangles keeps
the clustering--degree profile of x1 and reproduces the observed one.

\paragraph{Exploratory alternatives} (\code{playground/PR4-rcpp-engine/generacion\_de\_redes.pdf}).
An ERGM with GWESP could not be fitted with the race calibration: MCMLE did not converge,
stochastic approximation reached the GWESP target but destabilized the small groups
(Hispanics $+36$--$41\%$ off their targets; Asians bistable around a clique of 27 nodes),
and $\times 3$ degenerated with every method tried. \code{ergm.ego} with the GSS 2004
alter--alter ties did not converge either. The constrained rewiring keeps everything
calibrated exactly and is cheap; what it gives up is a probability model for x2.

\subsection{Held-out validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\code{scripts/09\_network\_validation.R} splits the 1{,}424 respondents of the GSS 2004
network module in two halves stratified by race, computes the targets from half A, builds
24 networks per level from half-B nodes, and compares them with the confidants of half B
(bootstrap of respondents and networks, $B = 1000$). Table and text for the paper in
\code{paper-NDFU/sections/}\allowbreak\code{network\_validation.tex}; figure in
\code{plots/09\_network\_validation/}. In short:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Homophily.} Out of sample, relative degree and same-race share fall inside
    the intervals for every group (all egos: 0.896 vs.\ 0.895 $[0.874, 0.914]$). Not
    targeted: same religion $-3\%$ and age difference $+6\%$ (inside); same sex $-5\%$
    $[-9, -1]$ and education difference $+9\%$ $[+2, +16]$ (small, outside). Random sets of
    $k$ contacts are 30\% more diverse in race than real confidants: the generator spreads
    cross-race ties evenly over egos.
  \item \textbf{Closure.} 80.5\% of half-B confidant pairs know each other, 42.8\% are
    especially close. Confidants are not a random draw from 30 contacts (random $k$: 8\%
    in x1, 16\% in x2). Taking the $k$ most embedded contacts (most common neighbours with
    the ego), x2 falls inside that band (63\%) and x1 at its lower edge (41\%); taking the
    $k$ nearest in Gower distance, 38\% and 25\%. x2 fits closure better than x1.
  \item \textbf{Profile.} Closure among confidants is flat to falling with the number of
    confidants ($5+$ minus $2$--$3$: $-4.7$ $[-11.7, +1.9]$ points); x1 and x2 agree under
    all three rules, x2g does not ($+16$ to $+28$; every interval excludes zero).
  \item \textbf{Not reproduced.} In the networks, closure follows homophily: the most
    embedded contacts are 70--80\% less diverse in race and religion than real confidants.
\end{itemize}
Caveat: the sex, religion, age and education coefficients come from Smith et al.\ (2014),
who used the GSS 1985 and 2004 confidants, so those features are not independent of
half B; the test is that the coefficients, the race calibration and 30 contacts together
give the confidant-level shares.

\subsection{Frozen gate and the engine}
\label{sec:frozengate}

\paragraph{The gate.} The pass probability of a tie is still
$w(d_{ij}) = \sigma((h - d_{ij})/0.02)$. Under the \emph{annealed} gate of
\S\ref{sec:premiumresult} every frontier tie drew a new coin at every step, so a tie with
small $w$ still passed now and then: with enough steps the gate only delayed adoption, and
the result depended on the step limit (200) and the patience (25); with 2000/400 the
premium fell 7--10\%. The \emph{frozen} gate draws one coin per tie and simulation, shared
by both directions: a tie is open or closed for the whole run, so the gate decides who
counts. After the draw the cascade is deterministic and stops at a fixed point (about 6.5
steps on average); no step limit or patience is needed. This is the percolation reading of
a stochastic threshold, in which thresholds count sources, not exposures (Centola \& Macy
2007, p.~707 and n.~20).

\paragraph{The engine} (\code{05\_unified\_engine.\{cpp,R\}}, Rcpp).
\code{run\_exposure\_grid()} runs the whole (MSP, IUL) grid of one network, topology,
$\lambda$ and exposure condition in one call (0.13--0.15\,ms per simulation on one core).
\code{05\_engine\_check.R}: (1) the annealed engine reproduces the former R loop of 05,
(2) the frozen engine an independent R cascade, (3) the sweep engine the frozen
single-simulation engine, each in 2{,}376 of 2{,}376 cells; (4) the exposure formula
equals hand values on a star ego; (5) every exposure condition equals an independent R
implementation cell by cell. \code{05\_netdiffuser\_reference.R}: netdiffuseR 1.26.0
(\code{rdiffnet} on the open ties, with an adoption mechanism that divides by the full
degree) gives the same number of adopters as the engine in 360 of 360 cells (about 200\,ms
against 0.15\,ms per simulation).

\paragraph{The DP twin} is rewired from the x2 network ($20m$ swaps, seed $500000 + k$)
and is the \emph{same} twin for x1 and x2, which have the same degrees: x2 $-$ x1 is a
paired comparison in which only the closure of the network changes. The seed cluster
($0.40 \times$ maximum degree, primary shared by the network and its twin for random,
central and marginal) and the simulation seeds are shared by x1 and x2 and by every
exposure condition. In the CSV files the network is \code{topology = "NET"} (it was
``GSS'' until 2026-11, also for ATP).

\subsection{The exposure axis (supplementary material)}
\label{sec:exposureaxis}

The main text uses the base exposure $E_i = a_i/d_i$ (adopting contacts behind open ties
over all contacts). The supplement varies how adopting contacts add up, with one formula
and two dials (``C1'' of \code{playground/PR3-similarity-vs-diversity/expresion\_unica.pdf}):
\begin{equation*}
  E_i = \frac{\sum_g A_\theta(a_{ig}) + 2\kappa\, e^{a}_{i}}{\sum_g A_\theta(n_{ig}) + 2\kappa\, e^{N}_{i}},
  \qquad A_\theta(x) = \frac{x}{1 + \theta (x - 1)},
\end{equation*}
where $g$ runs over the circles of $i$'s contacts (race $\times$ religion cells), $a_{ig}$
and $n_{ig}$ are the adopting (behind open ties) and all contacts of $i$ in circle $g$, and
$e^{a}_{i}$, $e^{N}_{i}$ the ties among adopting contacts and among all contacts of $i$.
Provisional names after Traag (2016): \textbf{independent reinforcement} $\theta$ (Kish's
discount of redundant sources in a circle) and \textbf{cohesive reinforcement} $\kappa$ (a
bonus for every pair of adopting contacts who know each other). $\theta = \kappa = 0$ is
the base. Both arms sit on one signed axis, $c$ = (value of the second adopter) $- 1$:
$c = -2\theta/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta$, $c = 2\kappa$ for $\kappa$. The sweep uses
$c = -1, -0.5$ ($\theta = 1, 1/3$), $0$, $+0.5, +1$ ($\kappa = 0.25, 0.5$), and every arm
level has a \textbf{permuted control} (same circle sizes, or the same number of pairs per
ego, with random members): real $-$ permuted is the part of the effect that comes from
\emph{where} the circles and pairs are. Results: \code{scripts/06c\_exposure\_axis.R}.

\paragraph{The reverse-premium corner} (robust DP wins: one-sided max-$T$ per strategy
$\times$ $\lambda$ panel, familywise 5\%, as in \S\ref{sec:premiumresult}). \textbf{GSS}: robust
at $\lambda = 0.5$--$0.7$ in all five strategies (39--44, 32--44 and 8--18 robust corner cells),

It is gone from $\lambda = 0.8$ (before: detectable but negligible there). \textbf{ATP}: only
small robust cells ($\le 0.3$ pp) at $\lambda \le 0.7$, although single cells reach 3--5 pp. The
corner stays a weak-coupling feature and, with these networks, a GSS one. A plausible reason
for the deeper corner is the frozen gate (not isolated here): a tie that is closed never opens, so where the gate is wide open and agents are
averse, homophily keeps the resistant among the resistant (the mechanism of
\S\ref{sec:premiumresult}).

\paragraph{What breaking the network destroys} (\code{07\_structural\_diagnosis.R}; means over
24 networks, GSS; ATP within a few percent). From x2 to x1 only closure changes: global and
local clustering halve ($0.149 \to 0.075$ and $0.151 \to 0.077$), degree assortativity falls
from 0.39 to 0.31, modularity from 0.40 to 0.37, and the homophily on the five axes stays
(race, sex and religion identical; age and education within 5\%). The DP twin removes the
rest: attribute assortativity drops to about 0 on every axis (race $0.69 \to 0.00$), the
mean Gower distance over ties rises by 57\% ($0.226 \to 0.355$), clustering falls to 0.039
(what the degree sequence alone produces), degree assortativity to 0 and modularity to
0.15. x2 $\to$ x1 $\to$ DP thus separates closure from homophily: closure moves the premium by
2--11\% (downward), and the premium itself goes with the homophily that only DP removes.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the level of closure: x3} (three times the triangles of every node:
option \code{--closure=3} of 02; base exposure, five strategies, the same DP twin; 06d adds
it when its sweep exists). The premium keeps falling as closure grows but stays
far from zero. GSS, random seeding, $\lambda$-mean: x1 7.14, x2 6.60, x3 5.79 pp $[5.45, 6.08]$
(x3 $-$ x1 $= -1.34$ $[-1.65, -1.08]$, $-19\%$); ATP: 7.15, 6.80, 6.13 pp $[5.54, 6.65]$
($-14\%$). Over the five strategies x3 is 10--20\% below x1. The validation does not pick
between x2 and x3 (both fall inside the GSS 2004 band with the embeddedness rule; x3 near
its upper edge), so the level of closure belongs to the limitations: a factor of 2 costs the
premium 2--11\%, a factor of 3 costs 10--20\%.

\paragraph{What the intervals cover now.} The nodes are resampled for every network, so the run
bootstrap covers the draw of agents from the survey's complete cases, the ties, the seed and
the dynamics. It does not cover the sampling error of the GSS 2004 targets, the uncertainty of
the coefficients of Smith et al.\ (2014), or the choice of the closure level (x2 vs.\ x3,
below). The link-free gap remains the statistic to lead with in the paper; $\beta_{\text{DP}}$
depends on the surface basis (\S\ref{sec:premiumresult}).

\paragraph{Still open.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The validation shows two small homophily misfits (same sex $-5\%$, education
    difference $+9\%$) and that the generator ties closure to homophily; neither is
    expected to change the sign of the premium, but both belong in the limitations.
  \item ``Other'' (1--2\% of the nodes) is not calibrated.
  \item The level of closure is a choice: x2 sits inside the GSS 2004 band only if
    confidants are the most embedded contacts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Still open}
\label{sec:pr5open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ELSOC ideology and religion misfit.} With single case-control coefficients, same
    ideology is 9--10\% below the confidants (18\% for left and right) and small religious groups
    are off by 30--90\%. Calibrating ideology as a second group dimension (activity by education,
    homophily by education and by ideology, Newton on both sets of targets; the rewiring already keeps
    the ideology counts) is the natural fix; it would change the ELSOC networks and require rerunning
    their sweep (about 1 h).
  \item The ELSOC corner sits at higher IUL than the GSS box; 06b and 06e report it over the whole
    plane, but the figures of the corner still draw the GSS box.
  \item GSS 2014 has no ideological homophily of its own: the ideology circles are a test of an
    attribute outside the gate only if the networks sort on it. A sensitivity with ELSOC's ideology
    coefficient (or the literature's) is open.
  \item The science-attitude index of GSS 2014 ($\alpha = 0.47$) is not an innovation case; ELSOC
    has no innovation items either, so ATP remains the only innovation sample.
  \item ELSOC nodes are an unweighted resample of the 2018 respondents (as GSS and ATP); the survey
    design (40 cities, refresh sample) is not used.
  \item \code{c22}--\code{c24} (participation of the respondent, family and friends in the movement
    they value most) are not used yet; they are the closest measure of perceived exposure.
\end{itemize}

\section{To verify before writing any of this into the paper}
\label{sec:verify}

\begin{itemize}
  \item[$\boxtimes$] Exact reinforcement functional form in Tur 2024:
    $u_t = u_0 \cdot (1/a_t)^{\gamma}$ (multiplicative, power-law).
    \emph{Resolved against both PDFs (2026-08-31):} Tur 2018 (PRE), Eq.~(1), uses the
    \textbf{same} form (there called MQR, $q_t^i = q_0^i (1/a_t^i)^{\gamma}$);
    $a_t^i \in \mathbb{N}$ is explicitly the number of \textbf{adopting neighbors}
    (a state count, not received recommendations), with the rule evaluated only once
    informed ($a \ge 1$). Both papers restrict $\gamma \in [0,1]$ --- justified in 2018
    by non-increasing marginal social influence (concavity), with the reductio that
    large $\gamma$ trivializes diffusion on their $k = 4$ lattices --- and sweep
    $\gamma$ as a free ``intensity'' parameter (2024, Fig.~5: $\gamma \in \{0, 0.5,
    1\}$; heatmaps over $[0,1]$). \textbf{No empirical calibration of $\gamma$ exists in
    either paper}; see the transportability caveat in \S\ref{sec:gammastatus}.
  \item[$\square$] Whether Tur 2024's homophily-rewiring $\rho$ has a formal analogue we
    should cite when claiming nesting (their $\rho$ $\approx$ our imputation; not
    identical constructs).
  \item[$\boxtimes$] Whether the additive composition has more than conventional support.
    \emph{Resolved 2026-11-10} (\S\ref{sec:mechanisms}): it is the best-response form of
    conformity and coordination games; learning does not give Tur's form; no study measures
    the cross-partial; the premium survives both complementary forms.
  \item[$\square$] The random-utility framing sentence for IUL (already drafted in
    \code{JOURNAL\_FIT\_ANALYSIS.md}, \S 4d) composes cleanly with the
    situational-utility framing: $U_i(t) = \Gamma + \lambda \Ee_i$ is a private valuation
    plus a social term --- this is Brock \& Durlauf (2001)'s decomposition
    (\S\ref{sec:brockdurlauf}), worth citing.
\end{itemize}
